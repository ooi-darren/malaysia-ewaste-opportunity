# Sources

Full citations for every figure used in this case study. See `DATA_DICTIONARY.md` for how each was used and its specific limitations.

## E-waste generation and formal collection

- **UN Global E-waste Monitor 2020** (UNITAR/ITU). Malaysia's 2019 e-waste generation figure (364,000 tonnes) is used in this analysis as cited by the secondary source below — the primary report itself was not independently accessed this session (the 2024 edition is gated behind an ITU registration form; the 2020 PDF was retrieved but could not be parsed with the tools available).
- **Malaysia Department of Environment (DOE)**. 2021 household e-waste collection figure (2,459 tonnes), used as cited by the secondary source below — not independently located and fetched from a DOE original release.
- **Monash Lens (2023).** "Understanding Malaysia's informal e-waste recycling sector." https://lens.monash.edu/understanding-the-informal-e-waste-recycling-sector-in-malaysia/ — the secondary source for both figures above, and for the informal-sector copper pricing context (~RM20/kg) discussed in Notebook 02.

## Metal content of printed circuit boards

- Bizzo, W.A.; Figueiredo, R.A.; de Andrade, V.F. (2014). "Characterization of Printed Circuit Boards for Metal and Energy Recovery after Milling and Mechanical Separation." *Materials*, 7(6), 4555–4566. Retrieved and read directly (open access, PubMed Central): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5455934/

## PCB share of total e-waste mass

- Multiple academic reviews on e-waste/PCB recycling economics, consulted via web search summaries rather than a single primary paper this session read directly. The commonly cited 3–6% range is used as a rough scaling assumption, not a precisely sourced figure — see `DATA_DICTIONARY.md` for this limitation stated in full.

## Metal prices

- Kitco.com — spot precious metals price page (gold, silver) and base metals page (copper). Fetched 27–28 August 2026.
